# Dead Signal Choir Heist Flavour Pack

Owner lane: `Creative Hub`
Requested by: `games-manager`
Date: `2026-05-03`
Applies to: `Neon District` Milestone 2 flagship route `Dead Signal Choir Heist`

## Purpose

Give the builder lane one compact source for shell framing, hostile posture, ghost-archive payoff, and debrief consequence copy without reopening route discovery.

## Tone anchor

This route should read as a premium recovery job, not a generic holdout. The player is not stealing random data. They are pulling a dead-signal ledger out of Choir Exchange before Glasshouse can bury the escrow trail and erase the witnesses who could prove who owned the bridge.

Writing rules:

- keep the language sharp, district-specific, and expensive
- make `Morrow Relay Co-op` sound like embattled infrastructure operators, not rebels for flavour
- make `Glasshouse` sound polished, extractive, and quietly cruel
- keep `Choir Exchange`, `signal bridge`, `ghost archive`, and `choir skyhook` in the vocabulary
- favour one hard consequence sentence over a pile of vague cyberpunk mood wording

## Story spine

### Client stakes

`Morrow Relay Co-op` needs the dead-signal ledger because it proves who has been rerouting Choir Exchange traffic into private escrow and who gets to erase the district's witness trail afterwards. If the ledger leaves with Glasshouse, Morrow loses the bridge, the route tariffs harden, and every courier in the lane starts paying for a silence they did not choose.

### Hostile posture

`Glasshouse Combine` is not defending territory for pride. It is defending a leverage asset. Their teams should read like witness erasers and polished debt collectors who would rather close a skyline than let one proof trail leave the district.

### Why the ledger matters

The dead-signal ledger is the receipt chain behind Choir Exchange traffic, hush escrow, and the bridge-side names nobody is meant to see. Stealing it does two things:

1. it gives Morrow proof that Glasshouse bought the bridge through coercion and erased the people who could testify
2. it turns a local contract into district-visible humiliation if the proof gets out clean

## Implementation-ready copy

### Shell mission sentence

Use this as the one-sentence contract read:

`Morrow Relay Co-op is paying you to break into Choir Exchange, pull the dead-signal ledger out of Glasshouse escrow, and escape through the choir skyhook before the witness erasers close the bridge.`

### Shell briefing copy

Use this for the selected-contract body or contract card detail:

`Glasshouse is about to seal the Choir Exchange signal bridge behind a dead-signal ledger that proves who bought the route and who vanished when the bridge changed hands. Morrow wants the proof trail out, the ghost archive cracked if the lane gives you room, and the skyline cleared before the erase teams turn the whole exchange into a clean lie.`

### Contract-board pressure line

Use this as the short pressure read:

`Client: Morrow Relay Co-op // Hostile: Glasshouse witness erasers // Prize: the ledger that can burn the bridge deal in public`

### Hostile posture line

Use this for HUD, dossier, or pre-run threat language:

`Glasshouse will trade bodies to keep the ledger quiet, but they will spend the real money on closing witnesses, locking the skyline, and killing the skyhook window before proof leaves the district.`

## Ghost archive copy

### Optional-objective reason to take it

`The ghost archive is where Glasshouse parked the witness cache that makes the ledger undeniable. Crack it and Morrow leaves with proof, not just theft. Skip it and the main contract still pays, but the district only gets half the story.`

### Optional-objective success note

`You lifted the witness cache before the bridge went loud. Now the ledger can name the deal and the archive can prove who got buried to close it.`

### Optional-objective skipped note

`You left the archive dark to keep tempo on the bridge. The ledger still matters, but the cleanest witness trail stayed under Glasshouse lock.`

## Debrief consequence language

### Success with archive

`Morrow left Choir Exchange with the dead-signal ledger and the ghost archive shard intact. Glasshouse did not just lose the bridge receipt; it lost the witness cache that kept the takeover deniable, and the district will feel that failure by morning.`

### Success without archive

`Morrow got the ledger off the signal bridge before the erase teams sealed the skyline. Glasshouse still lost the route proof, but the missing witness cache means Choir Exchange gets a hot victory instead of a total expose.`

### Failure after archive

`You cracked the witness cache, but Glasshouse killed the skyhook window before the proof could leave clean. For one bright minute the bridge had a voice, then the district watched it get strangled back into escrow.`

### Failure without archive

`Glasshouse shut the skyhook and forced the ledger back under escrow before Morrow could move it. Choir Exchange remembers the miss as another night where the bridge stayed bought and the witnesses stayed buried.`

## Screenshot-minded hero moments

Use these as the visual-writing targets when shell, debrief, or capture notes need one authored image to aim at:

1. Market-lane ascent: neon awnings, route props, and the first hint that this is a witness-theft job, not a random raid
2. Ghost archive pocket: the side detour should feel like stolen truth hidden in a cramped service wound off the main lane
3. Bridge hold: the signal bridge lit as a public nerve centre while Glasshouse crews try to retake the ring in halves
4. Choir skyhook finish: `Velvet Knife` as the final polished barrier between local proof and district broadcast

## Builder notes

- Keep `Morrow` framed as a pressured but necessary civic operator.
- Keep `Glasshouse` framed as elegant coercion, not cartoon villainy.
- Do not swap `ghost archive` for generic bonus-objective wording.
- If a shorter debrief is required, cut adjectives before cutting the consequence.
- The route should always answer three questions cleanly: who hired you, what proof did you steal, and what changed if you got out.
