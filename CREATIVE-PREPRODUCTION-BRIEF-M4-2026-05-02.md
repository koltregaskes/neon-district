## Asset brief

### Weapon and combat SFX families

- Ballistic shotgun:
  compressed close blast, heavy mechanical tail, short shell or chamber accent, strongest payoff at point-blank shield pressure.
- SMG:
  sharp synthetic clatter, lighter body than the shotgun, readable burst cadence, clearer suppression feel than raw damage weight.
- Energy weapon:
  rising capacitor whine, bright discharge, hotter sustain loop, obvious transition into overheat or cooldown states.
- Shield hit:
  hard electrical slap with a contained glassy shimmer so shielded enemies read instantly before the break.
- Shield break:
  brittle glass-electrical crack with a louder transient and a short debris fizz, matching `DESIGN-BIBLE.md`.
- Elite threat:
  low warning swell or servo surge before major attacks so the player can read escalation before the screen becomes busy.
- Extraction pressure:
  clean countdown pips, route-confirm stingers, and a distinct failure tone that feels operational rather than arcade-like.

### Ambient layers

- Transit layer:
  distant trains, rail hum, brakes, platform PA bleed.
- Industrial layer:
  vents, transformers, machinery pulses, servo knocks, rain on metal.
- Street pressure layer:
  gang chatter blur, sirens, power dips, neon buzz, signage crackle.
- Combat fallout layer:
  smoke venting, damaged wiring spit, debris ticks, distant alarms after heavy firefights.

These should be stemmed so the district can shift from exploration to alert to combat to extraction without hard resets.

### Music stem direction

- Exploration stem:
  sparse melancholy pad plus low industrial pulse.
- Alert stem:
  add clockwork percussion and nervous bass motion.
- Combat stem:
  distorted bass arp, harder transient percussion, denser high-end texture.
- Elite or boss escalation stem:
  extra sub pressure, harsher rhythmic punctuation, less melodic comfort.
- Extraction stinger:
  short triumphant but tense release, never fully safe or celebratory.

The score should intensify with danger instead of looping as a flat bed, matching the `DESIGN-BIBLE.md` system note.

## Browser-first Milestone 4 finish criteria

- Review routes at `README.md` URLs must stay deterministic and capture-ready.
- Each weapon family used in review footage must read through audio and VFX, not only UI numbers.
- Shield break, elite threat, and extraction states must be understandable with the HUD glanced at briefly, not studied.
- At least one screenshot set must show district signage, combat readability, and mission identity in the same frame.
- Ambience and music stems must support exploration, alert, combat, and extraction transitions without dead air or placeholder-feeling jumps.
- The flagship contract slice must provide one capture route that can be replayed end to end without obvious presentation placeholders.

## Recommended production order

1. Lock the weapon, shield-break, and extraction audio vocabulary first because it improves both play feel and capture quality.
2. Build ambience and music stems second so the district mood stops feeling flat between fights.
3. Stage the screenshot list against the featured review slices in `README.md`.
4. Only then do the key-art and trailer-beat framing pass, using real in-build visual proof instead of speculative concept art.
