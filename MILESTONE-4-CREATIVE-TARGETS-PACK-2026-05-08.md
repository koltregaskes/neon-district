## Route framing

Treat the featured route as a reviewable heist with three presentation states:

1. shell promise: the player is briefed into a high-risk district operation with clear intent
2. combat pressure: shield-breaks, elite pressure, and route collapse feel readable and authored
3. aftermath or extraction: the route resolves with operational tension, not arcade victory noise

Anything that does not strengthen one of those three states is out of scope for Milestone 4.

## Audio mood targets

### Weapon families

#### Shotgun

- emotional read: brutal close-quarters authority
- sound shape: compressed front-loaded blast, heavy metallic decay, short chamber or shell accent
- mix priority: must cut through shield pressure and smoke-heavy VFX without becoming harsh
- pass test: an observer should identify the shotgun as the heaviest weapon family within one exchange

#### SMG

- emotional read: agile suppression and rising panic
- sound shape: sharp synthetic clatter, lighter body than shotgun, fast burst cadence
- mix priority: should read as sustained pressure rather than raw stopping power
- pass test: the SMG should feel distinctly faster and more nervous than the shotgun in capture footage

#### Energy weapon

- emotional read: unstable high-voltage precision
- sound shape: rising capacitor whine into bright discharge with an obvious cooldown or release tail
- mix priority: must read above ambience during shield stripping and gadget-heavy moments
- pass test: the player or observer should hear the charge-to-discharge transition clearly enough to anticipate danger

### Combat state signatures

#### Shield hit

- emotional read: protected enemy confirmed
- sound shape: hard electrical slap with a contained glassy shimmer
- pass test: the first shielded contact should read from audio alone before the break lands

#### Shield break

- emotional read: exposed target and momentum swing
- sound shape: brittle glass-electrical crack with a louder transient and short debris fizz
- pass test: shield breaks should be the most satisfying state change in the route and never disappear into the combat bed

#### Elite arrival or pressure spike

- emotional read: the fight just became more dangerous
- sound shape: low warning swell, servo surge, or distorted rise before the major attack pattern starts
- pass test: an observer should feel escalation before they understand the exact enemy pattern

#### Extraction pressure

- emotional read: operational urgency, not celebration
- sound shape: clean countdown pips, route-confirm stinger, and a distinct failure or near-fail tone
- pass test: extraction should sound like a narrowing window, not a generic success state

### Ambience and music

#### Shell and briefing ambience

- target mood: cold premium machinery, terminal hum, district threat in the distance
- must include: subdued PA bleed, transformer hum, restrained neon buzz, soft rain or vent texture
- avoid: dead silence, bright menu cheer, or overly busy loops that compete with briefing copy

#### District traversal ambience

- target mood: layered industrial city pressure
- must include: distant trains, rail or vent hum, power dips, signage crackle, far sirens, rain on metal
- avoid: empty background beds that make the route feel like an arena with wallpaper

#### Combat stem

- target mood: distorted bass pressure and clockwork urgency
- must include: harder transients, darker pulse, enough headroom for shield-break and elite cues
- avoid: flat continuous loops that erase pacing changes

#### Extraction or aftermath stem

- target mood: tense release, never full safety
- must include: a short uplift or release signal with unresolved industrial tension under it
- avoid: triumphant arcade fanfare

## Hero-shot target list

Capture at least one keeper frame for each category below from the authored route.

### 1. Shell promise frame

- target shot: contract shell or briefing terminal with premium composition
- must show: route title, legible next action, strong signage or panel lighting, implied district depth
- ideal read: "this is a real operation with consequences"

### 2. District entry frame

- target shot: first step into the route with layered signage, weather, and route depth
- must show: player silhouette or route anchor, lighting contrast, one clear navigational line
- ideal read: "the city is dense, expensive, and hostile"

### 3. Shield-break combat frame

- target shot: mid-fight instant where shield collapse, weapon identity, and enemy separation are readable in one still
- must show: shield-break cue, player threat direction, one dominant weapon read
- ideal read: "combat looks dangerous but decipherable"

### 4. Elite pressure frame

- target shot: bright escalation beat with elite presence or major pressure spike
- must show: elite threat source, readable player position, VFX hierarchy that still preserves decision-making
- ideal read: "the route peaks with authored pressure instead of screen clutter"

### 5. Extraction countdown frame

- target shot: the route under time pressure as the exit or objective window closes
- must show: urgency, route clarity, timer pressure, and a readable path to resolution
- ideal read: "the heist is ending under stress, not dissolving into cleanup"

### 6. Aftermath frame

- target shot: smoke, debris, or district aftermath after a successful push
- must show: environmental consequence, reduced chaos, and enough negative space for future promo text if needed
- ideal read: "something costly just happened here"

## Signage and copy flavour notes

Use these as local flavour rules, not as a mandate to add more lore surfaces.

### Tone

- terse, operational, and urban
- imply debt, risk, surveillance, and extraction pressure
- prefer institutional menace over joke writing or genre parody

### Signage language

- good direction: route warnings, asset seizure language, transit authority notices, containment advisories, emergency reroute prompts
- avoid: comic-book slogans, meme language, or broad cyberpunk cliches that flatten the route into wallpaper

### Shell and route copy

- contract board or mission copy should stress consequence, payout tension, and hostile district conditions
- extraction-related copy should sound procedural and narrowing, not heroic
- aftermath language should reinforce cost, contamination, or unresolved faction pressure

### Short flavour examples

- `Transit authority reroute in effect. Choir traffic flagged hostile.`
- `Signal vault instability rising. Retrieval window collapsing.`
- `District mesh compromised. Exit corridor integrity not guaranteed.`
- `Asset handoff pending. Delay increases trace exposure.`

## Builder-ready execution order

1. Lock weapon, shield-hit, shield-break, elite, and extraction audio identities first.
2. Layer shell and district ambience so quiet moments stop feeling placeholder.
3. Stage the six hero-shot targets against the authored review route and reject any frame that only works by luck.
4. Apply concise signage and copy flavour only where it strengthens shell promise, combat pressure, or extraction tension.
5. Re-run the Milestone 4 checklist and note which gates improved from the added creative authorship.

## Verification notes

The implementing lane should mark this pack successful only if:

- at least one observer can distinguish shotgun, SMG, energy, shield-break, elite pressure, and extraction without reading labels first
- three of the six hero-shot categories produce captures worth keeping on the first controlled pass
- shell and extraction copy feel sharper without requiring new systems or broader narrative expansion
- the route feels more premium in stills and playback without depending on paid assets or external tools

## Risk

This pack sharpens the presentation target, but it does not replace the need for a real outsider-style playtest pass or a performance hotspot verdict on the review hardware path.
